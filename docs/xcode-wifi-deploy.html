Xcode WiFi Device Deployment
How to build and deploy Sorriva (or any Passione iOS app) directly to your iPhone over WiFi from the Mac Mini — no USB cable, no TestFlight, no App Store.
Overview
What this gives you
2-3 minute deploy cycle. Make a Swift change → scp to Mini → ⌘R in Xcode → app installs on your iPhone wirelessly. No TestFlight wait. No USB cable. Phone can be anywhere on your network.
One-time USB setup required. The first pairing must be done with the phone physically connected to the Mini via USB. After that, all future deployments are wireless.
Step 1 — One-time setup
Pair your iPhone to the Mac Mini
Prerequisites: iPhone and Mac Mini must be on the same WiFi network. Developer Mode must be enabled on the iPhone (Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode → On). Your Apple Developer account must be signed into Xcode.
1
Connect iPhone to Mac Mini via USB
Use the Lightning or USB-C cable. On the iPhone, tap Trust when the "Trust This Computer?" prompt appears and enter your passcode.
2
Open Devices and Simulators in Xcode
On the Mini: Xcode → Window → Devices and Simulators (or ⇧⌘2). Your iPhone should appear under Devices on the left.
3
Enable "Connect via network"
Click your iPhone in the device list. Check the box next to "Connect via network". A network icon (globe) will appear next to your device name within a few seconds.
4
Unplug the USB cable
Your iPhone should remain visible in the device list with a WiFi indicator. If it disappears, wait 10–15 seconds — the Mini is re-discovering it over the network.
5
Verify the wireless connection
In Xcode, select your iPhone from the device target picker (top toolbar, next to the scheme name). The globe icon confirms it's connected wirelessly. Hit ⌘R to confirm a build and install works.
Pairing persists. Once paired, your iPhone will reconnect automatically whenever it's on the same network as the Mini and Xcode is open. No need to repeat this process.
Step 2 — Daily dev workflow
Building and deploying wirelessly
1
Make sure Xcode is open on the Mini
The iPhone only shows as available when Xcode is running. It doesn't need to be in the foreground.
2
Deploy Swift files from Claude
Download the Swift file(s) from Claude. scp each one to the Mini:
scp ~/Downloads/ContentView.swift tomfo@100.126.165.63:~/projects/sorriva-app/ContentView.swift
3
Select your iPhone as the build target
In Xcode's top toolbar, click the device picker and select Tom's iPhone (should show a WiFi/globe icon, not a USB icon).
4
Build and run
Hit ⌘R. Xcode compiles the Swift code and installs the app directly on your iPhone over WiFi. Typically 1–3 minutes depending on what changed.
5
App launches automatically on your iPhone
The app opens on your phone. For subsequent changes, repeat from step 2. Xcode only recompiles changed files so subsequent builds are faster.
Troubleshooting
Common issues
ProblemFix
iPhone not showing in Xcode Make sure iPhone and Mini are on the same WiFi network. Open Xcode on the Mini. Wait 15–20 seconds for discovery. If still missing, plug in USB briefly to re-trigger the wireless connection, then unplug.
Globe icon missing, USB icon showing Plug in via USB, open Window → Devices and Simulators, re-check "Connect via network", unplug again.
"Could not find device" build error iPhone went to sleep or left the network. Wake the iPhone, ensure it's on WiFi, wait for Xcode to re-discover it (up to 30 seconds).
Build succeeds but app doesn't launch Tap the app icon on your iPhone manually. Sometimes the auto-launch fails over WiFi but the install succeeded.
Signing error on build Xcode → Preferences → Accounts — confirm your Apple ID is signed in. In the project target → Signing & Capabilities, confirm the team is set to RXKPBV2A4D (Thomas Fournier).
Developer Mode disabled after iOS update Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode → toggle On. iPhone will restart.

WiFi vs TestFlight summary: WiFi deploy (⌘R) is for active development — fast, direct, requires Xcode open on Mini. TestFlight is for sharing builds with others or final pre-release validation. For solo development on Sorriva, you should never need TestFlight — only use it when you want someone else to test.
